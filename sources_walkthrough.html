Bringing a story into conference

Moira:Desk treats sources differently. PA is wire copy written for newsrooms like ours to use: drag it in and it's ready to pitch. A BBC News or MEN story is someone else's published journalism: paste its link and pitch it as a follow-up, answering three questions before the producer can pick it. Choose a source, then watch, step through, or try it yourself.

Three kinds of source

How you bring a story in tells the newsroom, and your EP, where it came from. The EP wall and the report count every pitch by source.

PA PA wire

Agency copy written for newsrooms to use, under our PA licence.

Add it
Drag the headline from PA Explore onto the pitch box
Fills in
PA's slug, the headline line and the link
Marked
PA badge
Questions
None
Producer
Can pick it straight away
In the script
Starts empty; the PA link sits beside the pitch brief
Counts as
Wire

Follow-up · BBC BBC News / MEN

Someone else's published journalism. We can follow it up, but we need to know where it really came from.

Add it
Copy the link, paste it into the pitch box
Fills in
The headline and the link; you add the slug
Marked
FOLLOW-UP badge; the card stays red until answered
Questions
When it actually happened · the original source · what's new or our angle
Producer
Sees Pending until all three are answered
In the script
Starts with your three answers as [prompts]; delete them as you write
Counts as
Follow-up

Source Any other link

A link from anywhere else, kept with the pitch so the newsroom can check it.

Add it
Drag the link onto the pitch box (pasting it just types the address)
Fills in
The link, plus the link's text if you drag it from a page; you type the slug
Marked
A plain "Source" link
Questions
None

In the script
Starts empty; the link sits beside the pitch brief
Counts as
Original, like a typed-in pitch
Remember: PA, drag it in. BBC or MEN, paste the link, answer the three questions with the pencil, and delete the [prompts] from the script once it's written.
